## How the platform makes money

Not from forfeits. That is the model we are deliberately not building, and it is
the difference between this and the incumbent.

**Never:** a cut of forfeits, a volume-based fee from the recipient charity, or
yield on staked funds. All three are revenue derived from a user's bad day.

**Primary model: the premium coach.** The free tier returns a verdict. The paid
tier is the long-memory coach: encrypted session history on 0G Storage, patterns
recognised across months, exports. It is the one part of the product that gets
better the longer someone stays, and it is worth noting what that means for the
architecture: 0G Storage is not a nice-to-have here, it is the thing we sell.

**Later:** B2B seats, where organisations buy for their teams and route forfeits
to a cause they choose. The charity routing is what makes this reach CSR budget
alongside wellness budget, so the constraint becomes the selling point.

**Open question, not decided:** a small fee on successful refunds. Attractive
because it inverts the incumbent exactly, we would be paid when a user succeeds.
The concern is that it quietly rewards us for making sessions easy to pass, and
the narrative inversion is already available without it. Under review.

Beeminder's revenue arrives when a user fails. Ours arrives when someone keeps
paying for something that works. That is a durability argument as much as an
ethical one: a business that profits from failure has a ceiling on how much it is
allowed to help.
